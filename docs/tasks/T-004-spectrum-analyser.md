# T-004: SampleFifo, window functions and SpectrumAnalyser
- Owner: dsp-engineer
- Milestone: M1
- Status: DONE
- Depends on: T-002, T-003, T-021

## Goal
Implement the bin-domain half of the spectrum pipeline as pure, real-time-aware DSP classes in `source/dsp/`:
- a lock-free multichannel sample FIFO fed by the audio thread;
- calibrated window functions;
- a `SpectrumAnalyser` that frames the stream with overlap, combines channels according to the channel mode, runs the FFT, and keeps averaged power spectra and the secondary accumulators.

T-005 builds the display mapping on top of this.

## Design requirements
- **Namespace.** Use `investigate`. There must be no dependency on the processor, the editor or APVTS. Settings are passed in as a plain struct.
- **`SampleFifo`.**
  - Single producer (the audio thread) and single consumer (the analysis thread).
  - Up to 4 channels: main L/R and sidechain L/R. Main and sidechain may use two separate FIFO instances.
  - Capacity is fixed in `prepare` and must be at least 2^17 frames per channel.
  - `push` never blocks, allocates or throws. When there is not enough space, it drops the incoming block and increments an atomic overflow counter.
  - The consumer can detect an overflow and resynchronise.
- **FFT.**
  - Use pffft (marton78 fork). T-021 integrates it and makes it linkable from `source/dsp/` and the unit tests.
  - Put it behind a small real-FFT interface, with a `juce::dsp::FFT` implementation of the same interface kept as the correctness reference in tests.
  - Support sizes from 512 to 65536 (powers of two). pffft needs aligned buffers (`pffft_aligned_malloc`); allocate them only on the consumer thread.
  - Benchmark both implementations on the development machine at 4096 and 65536 points (real forward transform, Release). Record the numbers in the task log; this answers T-002 open question 1.
- **Windows.** Hann (default), Blackman (classic 3-term, a0 = 0.42), Hi-Res and flat-top.
  - Hi-Res is the square of the 4-term Nuttall window. Cite the Nuttall variant you use (Nuttall 1981, IEEE Trans. ASSP 29(1)). This matches SPAN's documented "Hi-Res (Nuttall squared)" window (T-001 section 2).
  - Apply coherent-gain correction so that a full-scale sine centred on a bin reads 0 dBFS.
- **Channel modes.** Mid = (L+R)/2, Side = (L−R)/2, Left, Right, L+R power mean = (|L|² + |R|²)/2, L/R dual and M/S dual. With a mono main input, every mode analyses the single channel. The sidechain is analysed with the same single-curve rule; in dual modes it uses the power mean of its channels.
- **Overlap.** Support 1x, 2x, 4x and 8x. The hop size is fftSize / overlap.
- **Averaging.** `avgTime` follows SPAN's convention: it is the time for the displayed level to fall by 20 dB (roadmap parameter constraints; T-001 section 2).
  - Use exponential averaging in the power domain with τ = avgTime / ln(100).
  - The per-frame coefficient is α = 1 − exp(−hopSeconds / τ). This keeps the behaviour independent of fftSize, overlap and sample rate.
  - `avgTime = 0` means no averaging.
- **Secondary accumulators.** Each works per bin, per curve:
  - Real-time max: holds the per-bin maximum of the averaged spectrum and falls linearly in dB at 20 dB per `avgTime`. With `avgTime = 0` it equals the current frame. There is no fixed release constant.
  - All-time max: never decreases until reset.
  - All-time average: the cumulative mean in the power domain.
  A `resetSecondary()` call clears them. The analyser also clears them itself whenever `channelMode`, `fftSize`, `window` or the sample rate changes (roadmap Decisions).
- **Reallocation.** Changes to the settings struct (fftSize, window, overlap, channel mode, sample rate) are applied on the consumer thread. Reallocation is allowed there. The producer API stays real-time safe at all times.
- **Clean output.** Digital silence must never produce NaN or Inf. Clamp powers to a floor equivalent to −200 dB.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] The pffft and `juce::dsp::FFT` implementations agree bin for bin, within 1e-4 relative error in power, on white noise at 512, 4096 and 65536 points. The benchmark numbers for both are in the task log.
- [ ] A sine at −6.00 dBFS centred on a bin reads −6.00 ±0.05 dB with Hann, Blackman and Hi-Res at fftSize 4096 and 65536.
- [ ] The Hi-Res window's leakage 20 bins away from a centred full-scale sine is ≤ −120 dB relative to the peak, at fftSize 65536. The −120 dB bound is set by float32 FFT precision, not by the window's theoretical sidelobes. The log states the theoretical sidelobe level with a citation.
- [ ] With flat-top, a sine at −6.00 dBFS at any frequency between bins reads −6.00 ±0.02 dB.
- [ ] Hann scalloping loss, with the sine exactly between two bins, is 1.42 ±0.1 dB.
- [ ] With L = R (a sine), the Side power is ≤ −150 dB. With L = −R, the Mid power is ≤ −150 dB. Left and Right modes isolate their channel: the other channel's sine is ≤ −150 dB.
- [ ] Frame count: for N input samples, fftSize F and hop H, the number of frames produced is floor((N − F) / H) + 1, ±1, for all overlaps.
- [ ] Averaging: after a steady sine stops, the averaged level at its bin has fallen by 20 dB (±0.5 dB) at t = avgTime (±5 %). Tested for avgTime values of 100, 1000 and 5000 ms, at 44.1 and 96 kHz, and at overlaps of 2x and 8x.
- [ ] Real-time max: after a sine stops, the max curve at that bin falls linearly in dB at 20 dB per `avgTime` (±5 %), for avgTime values of 500 and 2000 ms. All-time max never decreases until reset. All-time average of a sine that plays for 1 s, followed by 1 s of silence, reads −3.01 ±0.1 dB relative to the sine's level.
- [ ] Changing `channelMode`, `fftSize`, `window` or the sample rate clears all secondary accumulators by the next frame. Changing `avgTime`, `overlap` or `secondary` does not clear them.
- [ ] No NaN or Inf appears in any output for silence, full-scale white noise, a DC input, or denormal-range input.
- [ ] `SampleFifo`: in a 10 s stress test with the producer and consumer on separate threads, data arrives in order with no corruption. The overflow counter increments exactly when the consumer is paused and the FIFO is full.
- [ ] `SampleFifo::push` performs no allocation (verified by unit-tester's allocation check).
- [ ] Performance, Release, on the development machine: processing 1 s of stereo 48 kHz audio at fftSize 4096 and 4x overlap takes ≤ 15 ms. At fftSize 65536 and 8x overlap, in a dual mode, it takes ≤ 60 ms.
- [ ] Warning-clean at `/W4`. `docs/qa/T-004-unit.md` reports PASS.

  - **SampleFifo**: wraps one `juce::AbstractFifo` (index pair) over N parallel `std::vector<float>` channel arrays (sized once in `prepare`, never resized). `push()` checks `getFreeSpace()`; if less than the incoming block, drops the whole block and does `overflowCount.fetch_add(1, relaxed)` — no partial writes, matching "producer never blocks or allocates". `pop()` mirrors with `prepareToRead`/`finishedRead`. Both are plain index/memcpy work, no allocation after `prepare`.
  - **WindowFunctions**: periodic form (divisor `N`, not `N-1` — Harris 1978's distinction between analysis and filter-design windows), computed in `double`, stored as `float`. Periodic form is required, not just conventional: it keeps each window's DFT a finite sum of impulses at a few integer bin offsets, which is what makes a bin-centred sine's leakage land in only 1-3 neighbouring bins (needed for the Hi-Res −120 dB leakage acceptance test) and what makes the CG-normalisation exact for a bin-centred tone.
    - Hann: `0.5 − 0.5cos(2πn/N)`.
    - Blackman (classic 3-term, a0=0.42): `0.42 − 0.5cos(2πn/N) + 0.08cos(4πn/N)`.
    - Nuttall 4-term (Nuttall, A., "Some windows with very good sidelobe behavior," IEEE Trans. ASSP 29(1), pp.84-91, 1981; coefficients as reproduced by `scipy.signal.windows.nuttall`): `0.3635819 − 0.4891775cos(2πn/N) + 0.1365995cos(4πn/N) − 0.0106411cos(6πn/N)`. Peak sidelobe ≈ −93 dB (single window). Hi-Res = this window squared, cited in Voxengo's own docs as "Nuttall squared" (T-001 §2/9); squaring in the time domain convolves the spectrum with itself, so the sidelobe floor is theoretically ≈ −186 dB — the acceptance criterion's −120 dB bound is float32 FFT precision, not the window's own floor, exactly as the task states.
    - Flat-top (5-term "SFT5F", Heinzel, Rüdiger & Schilling, "Spectrum and spectral density estimation by the DFT...", 2002, also reproduced by `scipy.signal.windows.flattop`): `0.21557895 − 0.41663158cos(2πn/N) + 0.277263158cos(4πn/N) − 0.083578947cos(6πn/N) + 0.006947368cos(8πn/N)`.
    - `generate()` returns the coherent gain (mean of the raw window samples) alongside filling the array; `SpectrumAnalyser` folds this into its own bin-scale constant rather than pre-dividing the window (mathematically identical, avoids an extra per-sample pass).
  - **RealFft**: interface `forward(timeDomain[fftSize], outReal[bins], outImag[bins])`, unscaled, one-sided (`bins = fftSize/2+1`, DC/Nyquist real-only by contract). `PffftRealFft` copies into `pffft_aligned_malloc`'d input/output/work buffers (pffft requires 16-byte-aligned pointers), calls `pffft_transform_ordered(..., PFFFT_REAL, PFFFT_FORWARD)`, and unpacks pffft's packed format (`out[0]`=DC re, `out[1]`=Nyquist re packed into the "DC imag" slot per pffft.h, `out[2k]/out[2k+1]` = bin k re/im for `k=1..N/2-1`) into the plain `outReal/outImag` arrays. `JuceRealFft` copies into a `2*fftSize` scratch vector, calls `performRealOnlyForwardTransform(scratch.data(), true)`, and reads `scratch[2k]/scratch[2k+1]`. Both allocate only in their constructor (recreated by `SpectrumAnalyser::setSettings` on the analysis thread when `fftSize` changes — reallocation is allowed there per the task).
  - **SpectrumAnalyser power scaling** (full derivation, so the −6 dBFS/scalloping/Hi-Res-leakage acceptance numbers are traceable): for a full-scale sine exactly on bin k (1 ≤ k ≤ N/2−1), an unscaled real FFT of the CG-window-applied frame has magnitude `(1/2)·N·CG` at that bin (CG = mean of the raw window). So amplitude estimate `A[k] = (2/(N·CG))·|X[k]|` for interior bins, `A[k] = (1/(N·CG))·|X[k]|` for k=0 (DC) and k=N/2 (Nyquist) — no factor of 2, they are not mirrored. Power is `A[k]^2` (linear, 1.0 == 0 dBFS). Two scalar constants (`binScale`, `dcNyquistScale`) are computed once per `setSettings` call and applied per-bin in `float`; power is clamped to a floor of `1e-20` (−200 dB) before it ever reaches an averaged/secondary array, so silence/denormal/DC/white-noise inputs can never produce NaN/Inf downstream.
  - **Framing**: one shared `writePos` (bitmask-wrapped, fftSize is always a power of two) advances across up to 4 ring buffers (`ringMain[0/1]`, `ringSidechain[0/1]`, each pre-sized at the 65536-point maximum in `prepare()` so no fftSize change ever reallocates a ring — only the FFT plan and window table are recreated). Samples are combined **per input sample**, not per block, specifically so a single large `pushBlock` call (e.g. the acceptance test pushing N samples in one call) produces the same sequence of distinct, correctly-offset overlapping frames as many small calls would — combining only at the end of a call would collapse multiple hops' worth of frames into repeats of the same "current" window. A countdown counter (`fftSize` initially, `hopSize` thereafter, no modulo per sample) triggers `produceFrame()` inline.
  - **Channel combine** (per roadmap + T-004 spec): mono main input forces `bufferA = mono` for every `channelMode` (no second buffer, `isDual()` always false) per "every mode analyses the single channel". Stereo: `mid`/`side`/`left`/`right` combine to a single time-domain `bufferA`, one FFT, one exposed curve. `lrPowerMean` fills raw `bufferA=L`, `bufferB=R`, runs two FFTs, and combines **post-FFT in the power domain** (`0.5·(P_L+P_R)`) into one exposed curve — this is why it needs two buffers even though it isn't a "dual" mode (a time-domain (L+R)/2 average would just be Mid again, which is a different, already-named mode). `lrDual`/`msDual` also run two FFTs but expose both curves separately (`isDual()==true`). Sidechain is always a single exposed curve: 1ch is used directly, 2ch is combined post-FFT with the same power-mean rule as `lrPowerMean`, independent of the main `channelMode` — matches "the sidechain is analysed with the same single-curve rule; in dual modes it uses the power mean of its channels" and the roadmap's SpectrumFrame note.
  - **Averaging**: `τ = avgTimeSeconds / ln(100)`, `α = 1 − exp(−hopSeconds/τ)` per hop; `avgTimeMs ≤ 0` bypasses the blend entirely (`avg = rawFramePower`, matching "no averaging"/"equals the current frame" verbatim). Each curve's averaged array (`primary`/`primaryB`/`sidechain`) is "primed" (hard-set, not blended) on its first frame after a reset, so long `avgTime` values don't cause a slow attack ramp from the −200 dB floor before the accuracy tests' first reading.
  - **Secondary accumulators** read the **already-averaged** power (`c.avg[k]`, the roadmap's own pipeline order: averaging → secondary), not the raw per-frame power:
    - Real-time max: `rtMax[k] = avgTimeMs≤0 ? avg[k] : max(avg[k], rtMax[k]·decay)`, `decay = 10^(−2·hopSeconds/avgTimeSeconds)` — chosen so `10·log10(decay)` per hop accumulates to exactly −20 dB over one `avgTimeSeconds` of continuous decay, independent of `fftSize`/`overlap`/sample rate (all folded into `hopSeconds`).
    - All-time max: `allTimeMax[k] = max(allTimeMax[k], avg[k])`, only touched by `resetSecondary()`.
    - All-time average: Welford running mean in the power domain (`allTimeAvg[k] += (avg[k]-allTimeAvg[k])/count`), avoids an unbounded accumulator.
    - Only `primary`/`primaryB` get secondary accumulators (3 arrays each); the task's named bin-domain arrays are exactly `primary, primaryB, secondary, secondaryB, sidechain` — no secondary accumulator for the sidechain curve.
  - **Reset semantics**: public `reset()` clears framing (ring/writePos/countdown) and re-primes the primary/primaryB/sidechain averaged arrays, but leaves the secondary accumulators alone — for `SampleFifo` overflow resync, where the displayed max/average history shouldn't be thrown away over a dropped-samples glitch. Public `resetSecondary()` clears only `rtMax`/`allTimeMax`/`allTimeAvg` (+count), matching the task's literal wording. Internally, `setSettings()` calls **both**, plus re-primes the averaged arrays, whenever `channelMode`, `fftSize`, `window`, `sampleRate`, or the main/sidechain channel counts differ from the previous settings (channel-count changes aren't in the task's explicit trigger list, but are treated the same way as a host bus-layout reconfiguration, since old ring-buffer content would otherwise mix samples combined under a different channel count). `overlap`, `avgTime` and `secondary` changes touch none of this state, matching the task's explicit "does not clear them" list.
  - **API surface for T-005** (no data is copied on every frame; accessors return raw pointers into persistent buffers, valid until the next `pushBlock`/`setSettings`/`reset`): `getPrimaryPower()`, `getPrimaryBPower()`, `getSidechainPower()`, `getRealTimeMaxPower(bool secondCurve)`, `getAllTimeMaxPower(...)`, `getAllTimeAveragePower(...)`, `getSecondaryPower(...)` (selects by `settings.secondary`, `nullptr` if `Off`), `isDual()`, `hasSidechain()`, `getNumBins()`, `getBinFrequency(bin)`.
